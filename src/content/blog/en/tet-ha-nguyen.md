When the last rice crop of the year has just been harvested, many Vietnamese families have a special meal: a pot of rice cooked from the new grain and a plate of new sticky rice, first placed on the altar, then shared by the family. That occasion is usually tied to **the full moon of the tenth month**, known as **Tết Hạ nguyên**.

## When it falls

Tết Hạ nguyên is the 15th day of the tenth lunar month. The next three dates, in Vietnam time:

| Lunar year | Lunar date | Solar date |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bính Ngọ | 15/10 | Monday, 23 November 2026 |
| Đinh Mùi | 15/10 | Friday, 12 November 2027 |
| Mậu Thân | 15/10 | Thursday, 30 November 2028 |

It is also a full-moon day (rằm), so what the post [Rằm and mùng một](/en/blog/ram-va-mung-mot/) says about working out the full-moon day applies here too.

## Meaning: giving thanks after the harvest

The full moon of the tenth month comes just after the rice harvest. That is why the sources all call it the **new-rice festival** (Tết cơm mới, tết lúa mới). Rice from the new crop is used to thank heaven, earth and the ancestors for a good harvest.

## Common customs

- Families cook rice, steam sticky rice or make cakes from the new rice and glutinous rice to offer to the ancestors.
- After the offering, the family sits down together to the new-rice meal.

## Common offerings

The tray centres on the new grain: **rice, sticky rice and cakes** made from the freshly harvested crop. The sources do not give one fixed tray; each family uses what the season brings.

## Different places, different days

The new-rice offering is not held on the same day everywhere:

- Some places hold it on the 15th of the tenth month.
- Some hold it on the 1st of the tenth month.
- Some hold it on the 10th of the tenth month, that is [Tết Trùng thập](/en/blog/tet-trung-thap/).

No day is "more correct". If your village or family has its own day, keep to it.
