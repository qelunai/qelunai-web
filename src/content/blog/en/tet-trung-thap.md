Beside the big festivals everyone knows, the Vietnamese lunar year has a few small ones that fewer people remember. The 10th day of the tenth month is one of them: **Tết Trùng thập** (Double Tenth). The name joins "trùng" (repeated) and "thập" (ten), because day ten meets month ten.

## When it falls

Tết Trùng thập is the 10th day of the tenth lunar month. The next three dates, in Vietnam time:

| Lunar year | Lunar date | Solar date |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bính Ngọ | 10/10 | Wednesday, 18 November 2026 |
| Đinh Mùi | 10/10 | Sunday, 7 November 2027 |
| Mậu Thân | 10/10 | Saturday, 25 November 2028 |

The Vietnamese lunar calendar is worked out in Vietnam time (UTC+7), so it is sometimes one day off from calendars printed elsewhere. The post [Rằm and mùng một](/en/blog/ram-va-mung-mot/) explains why.

## A festival for healers

The articles on this day all repeat one idea: according to the book *Dược lễ*, around the 10th of the tenth month medicinal plants are at their best, so the day is seen as **the festival of traditional healers**. We have not read *Dược lễ* ourselves; this point is reported as the sources at the end of the post give it.

## Is it a new-rice festival?

Here the sources differ:

- In some places, people offer **new rice** (rice from the fresh harvest) on the 10th of the tenth month itself.
- In other places, the new-rice offering is made on **the 15th of the tenth month**, that is [Tết Hạ nguyên](/en/blog/tet-ha-nguyen/), five days later.

One source also calls the 10th of the tenth month "Tết Thường tân". The others all use the name Trùng thập, so this post does too.

If your family has its own custom for this day, keep to it. The sources we compared do not describe one shared ritual or offering tray for Tết Trùng thập, so this post does not list offerings.

## One of the "double" days

Trùng thập belongs to the group of days whose day and month numbers are the same, like the 5th of the fifth month ([Đoan Ngọ](/en/blog/tet-doan-ngo/)), the 7th of the seventh month ([Qixi](/en/blog/that-tich/)) and the 9th of the ninth month ([Double Ninth](/en/blog/tet-trung-cuu/)).
