A week before Lunar New Year, on **the 23rd of the twelfth month**, many Vietnamese families hold the offering for ông Công ông Táo, also called seeing off the Kitchen Gods (Táo quân).

## When it falls

The Kitchen Gods day is the 23rd of the twelfth lunar month (tháng Chạp). The next three dates, in Vietnam time:

| Lunar year | Lunar date | Solar date |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bính Ngọ | 23/12 | Saturday, 30 January 2027 |
| Đinh Mùi | 23/12 | Wednesday, 19 January 2028 |
| Mậu Thân | 23/12 | Tuesday, 6 February 2029 |

Six days later (seven when the twelfth month has 30 days) comes [New Year's Eve](/en/blog/giao-thua/).

## The story of the Kitchen Gods

In Vietnamese folk belief, on the 23rd of the twelfth month the Kitchen Gods go up to heaven to report to the Jade Emperor on what happened in the household during the year. The family holds a send-off and hopes for a calm new year.

The story says the Kitchen Gods ride a carp to heaven. That is why carp are an essential part of this offering.

## Common customs

- Families hold an offering to see the Kitchen Gods off.
- Afterwards, many release the carp into a pond or lake.

When releasing fish, choose clean water, let them go gently, and do not throw the plastic bag into the water.

## Common offerings

The items the sources agree on:

- **Carp**.
- **Kitchen God hats and robes**: three sets, two for the gods and one for the goddess.
- **Betel and areca** and **fruit**.

Beyond this, each family and region adds its own dishes. The sources do not agree on these, so this post does not list them.
