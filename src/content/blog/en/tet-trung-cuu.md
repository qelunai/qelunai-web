A few days in the lunar year have a day number that matches the month. The 9th of the ninth month is one of them: **Tết Trùng cửu**, "double nine".

## When it falls

Tết Trùng cửu is the 9th day of the ninth lunar month. The next three dates, in Vietnam time:

| Lunar year | Lunar date | Solar date |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Đinh Mùi | 9/9 | Friday, 8 October 2027 |
| Mậu Thân | 9/9 | Thursday, 26 October 2028 |
| Kỷ Dậu | 9/9 | Tuesday, 16 October 2029 |

The Vietnamese lunar calendar is worked out in Vietnam time (UTC+7), so it is sometimes one day off from calendars printed elsewhere. The post [Rằm and mùng một](/en/blog/ram-va-mung-mot/) explains why.

## Meaning

The number nine is associated with **long life**, so the day when two nines meet carries a wish for longevity.

## Origin and old customs

Tết Trùng cửu comes from China. According to old tradition, on this day people **climbed to a high place** and **drank chrysanthemum wine**.

The sources we compared do not describe a set of offerings or a specific rite for Trùng cửu in Vietnam, so this post does not list any.

## The "double" days

Trùng cửu belongs with the 5th of the fifth month ([Tết Đoan ngọ](/en/blog/tet-doan-ngo/)), the 7th of the seventh ([Thất tịch](/en/blog/that-tich/)) and the 10th of the tenth ([Tết Trùng thập](/en/blog/tet-trung-thap/)).
